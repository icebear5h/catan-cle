# Qwen3.8 27B Hex-Direction Probe

Date: 2026-08-16

## Question

Can Qwen3.8-27B reliably resolve one-hop relative positions on a pointy-top hex grid when atlas lookup and Catan object recognition are removed?

The six engine directions are presented in screen-relative language:

| Engine direction | Probe label |
| --- | --- |
| `WEST` | `LEFT` |
| `EAST` | `RIGHT` |
| `NORTHWEST` | `UP-LEFT` |
| `NORTHEAST` | `UP-RIGHT` |
| `SOUTHWEST` | `DOWN-LEFT` |
| `SOUTHEAST` | `DOWN-RIGHT` |

Pure `UP` and `DOWN` are not one-hop neighbors on a pointy-top hex grid, so the probe uses the four diagonal variants.

## Probe design

- Six 512×512 images, each with center hex `X` and six numbered neighbors.
- The candidate labels are Latin-square permuted: every number occupies every direction exactly once.
- Colors, scale, and center offsets vary across layouts.
- Two inverse tasks per neighbor:
  1. direction → numbered hex;
  2. numbered hex → direction.
- 36 questions per task, 72 total.
- Every direction and every numbered label is balanced.
- Ground truth comes from `engine.models.coordinate_system.UNIT_VECTORS` and the frontend-compatible pointy-top projection.
- No board contract, atlas prompt, filename clue, or textual topology is supplied.
- Chance accuracy is 1/6 (16.67%).

## Configuration

- Model: `qwen/qwen3.8-27b`
- Suite: isolated visual probe
- Reasoning: disabled; zero reasoning tokens reported
- Temperature: 0
- Concurrency: 2
- OpenRouter routing: unpinned; direct provider identity was not persisted. Completion-price fingerprints are consistent with 58 Chutes-FP8-rate requests and 14 AkashML-BF16-rate requests, but that endpoint attribution is inferred rather than directly observed.

## Confusion pattern

All six mistakes occurred in direction-to-label lookup, and every error collapsed a diagonal onto its horizontal neighbor:

- `UP-RIGHT → RIGHT`: 3
- `UP-LEFT → LEFT`: 2
- `DOWN-LEFT → LEFT`: 1

There were no cross-side errors, vertical inversions, random labels, malformed answers, or errors in the inverse label-to-direction task.

This is substantially cleaner than the full-board benchmark. On these six synthetic labeled layouts, Qwen3.8 distinguishes the six relative regions and names a visible label's relation perfectly. Its weaker operation is scanning from a verbal diagonal instruction to the correct visual cell; it occasionally stops at the same-side horizontal cell.

## Interpretation

**Strength:** The result is evidence against a fundamental inability to recognize one-hop hex directions: in this clean labeled setting, Qwen3.8 performs far above chance and perfectly handles label-to-direction classification.

**Remaining weakness:** Direction-conditioned visual selection is asymmetric and less reliable for upper diagonals. More importantly, this probe deliberately removes the harder operations that failed in CatanBoardBench: locating atlas tokens on a dense board, distinguishing pieces, and binding topology to canonical node/edge IDs. Six synthetic layouts cannot establish how much each of those factors—including direction handling—causes the full-board score.

A useful next rung would retain this balanced design but place the labels on actual Catan tiles, then remove the labels and identify neighbors by visible resource-number pairs.
